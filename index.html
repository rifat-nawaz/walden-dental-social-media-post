We borrow

  • Hook-first headlines and short, scannable captions
  • Numbered listicles and “save this” carousels
  • Named recurring series that followers recognize
  • Patient questions and objections in their own words
  • Myth vs fact cards with a source
  • Review quote cards and before/after stories
  • A consistent branded 4:5 template
  • “Comment below” and “Save this” prompts

We skip

  • “Best dentist in NYC” claims we can't prove
  • Hard-sell promos and “spots are limited” pressure
  • AI-looking imagery presented as real
  • Long hashtag dumps
  • Reels, video and team faces (not available this year)
  • Daily posting with thin content
  • Medical advice, medication or treatment instructions

Posting guidelines

Rules for everyone who posts

HIPAA first
  • Before/after Smile Stories only with a signed HIPAA authorization that covers social media. Crop to teeth and lips, no names, initials or dates. If no authorized case is ready, run the written fallback post.
  • No other real patients in any image. Lifestyle photos use stock or AI models that are never presented as patients.
  • Never post X-rays, charts, appointment screens or anything with a name, date of birth or treatment detail.
  • Never confirm that someone is a patient. When people comment or review, reply with general thanks and invite them to call the office. Don't mention their visit or treatment.
  • Review cards quote only lines already shown on waldendentalny.com that mention no treatment. No reviewer names, initials, photos or star graphics. Confirm the practice is comfortable reusing them.
  • Answer questions with general information only. Move anything personal to a phone call.
No medical advice
The social media manager is not a dentist, so posts share only general, sourced information: no diagnosis, no treatment instructions for symptoms, no medication or sedation information. When someone describes a symptom or asks about their own case in comments or messages, reply: “Thanks for asking! Every mouth is different, so please call us at (845) 713-4822 and we'll take a look.”
Brand voice
Warm, friendly and plain-spoken, like a neighbor who happens to be a dentist. Honest, never pushy: no fake urgency, no “limited time” deals, no fear tactics. Explain dental terms in everyday words. Light emoji on Facebook and Instagram, almost none on LinkedIn.
Who we serve
Teens and adults. Don't use young-child imagery or “kids' dentist” language. Clear braces are offered case by case, so they are not promoted in this calendar.
One image size for every post
  • Every image and every carousel slide: 1080 × 1350 px, 4:5 portrait, exported as JPG or PNG in sRGB. This is the size the top New York accounts use; it takes up more of the feed than a square.
  • Instagram and Facebook: single image or carousel. LinkedIn: the same slides as a PDF document carousel. X: slide 1 only.
  • Images only: no Reels, video or Stories this year.
  • Keep text and logo inside a 60 px margin; on Instagram keep key text out of the top and bottom 75 px, which the 3:4 profile grid crops.
  • Headlines in Fraunces, supporting text in Figtree, at least 36 px so they read on a phone.
Images
Use the image prompt in each topic for licensed stock or AI-generated visuals, then add the on-image text in design. Koppelman Dental's AI graphics got 3–11 likes, so keep AI images photorealistic and simple, and never present them as the real office or real patients. People shown must be models. Dr. Sayeed appears only through the portrait already on the website. No team photos and no video. Brand colors: navy #163a7a, accent blue #0870b8, sky blue #5fb0e8, ivory #f5f7fb, with emergency red #c8141c only for emergency posts. No star graphics next to reviews.
Facts you can use
Only these, as the website states them: Dr. Sayeed has performed more than 4,000 root canals and placed more than 100 dental implants; DDS from University of the Pacific; residency at Brookdale Hospital Medical Center; same-day emergency care usually available on weekdays, by appointment (call first, no walk-ins); CareCredit and Cherry financing for qualified applicants; HSA and FSA cards accepted. Never hard-code a Google review count or rating. Don't invent prices, ratings or outcomes.
Claims to avoid
  • No “filling fast” or “only a few spots left” unless the office confirms it that day. Use “Call to check availability.”
  • HSA is a payment method only. HSA balances carry over, so they're never part of “use it or lose it”.
  • FSA: “Some FSA funds may expire at year-end; your plan may allow a carryover or grace period.”
  • Insurance: “Many dental insurance benefits reset at year-end. Check your plan.” Never “most plans cover two cleanings”.
  • Say “performed more than 4,000 root canals”, not “successful”.
Website fixes to make
  • Implant lifespan: the About page says “20+ years” but the Implants page says “15 to 25 years or longer”. This calendar uses the Implants page wording. Pick one and use it everywhere.
  • The About page says “successful root canals”. Consider “performed” for consistency.
  • Some directories still list 57 Main St under older names (Dr. Angela Barrett, Young Dental) and as pediatric. Update them to match the website.

Placeholders to fill
Replace [Google Review Link] with the practice's Google review URL, and add the social handles when they're set up. Use “link in bio” on Instagram, where captions can't hold live links.
Posting times (ET)
Tuesday and Thursday: LinkedIn 8:30 AM, X 9:00 AM, Facebook 10:00 AM, Instagram 12:00 PM. Saturday: LinkedIn 9:00 AM, Facebook 10:00 AM, X 10:30 AM, Instagram 11:00 AM. These are starting points; after four weeks, move each slot to the time each platform's insights show works best.
Hashtags
Instagram: 6 to 8 per post, mixing local (#WaldenNY #HudsonValley #OrangeCountyNY), brand (#WaldenDentalNY) and topic tags. Facebook: none or one or two. LinkedIn: three at the end. X: one or two.
Approval workflow
Design visuals one week ahead. Send each week's 12 posts to the client for approval by the Thursday before. Status steps: Drafted, Designed, Client review, Approved, Scheduled, Published.
After posting
Reply to comments within one business day using the HIPAA reply rule. Every Monday, note reach, saves, link clicks and calls for the previous week, so December can lean into what works.
